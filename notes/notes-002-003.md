# Notes — 002-003

## Interpretation

Preserve the paired contours from experiment 002-002 and rotate the entire upper line clockwise by approximately 10 degrees. Use the upper curve's object origin near the center of the composition as the pivot so the rotation simultaneously raises the left side and lowers the right side, testing whether a simple rigid transformation can widen the launch opening and tighten the finish.

## Observations

The rotation strongly achieves the requested spacing contrast. The left opening becomes much wider and more dramatic, while the lines rapidly converge toward the right. The result has an energetic retro-futurist wedge silhouette and clearly demonstrates that rotation is an effective control for the overall taper.

At 10 degrees, however, the closure is too aggressive for a clean pair of logo boundaries: the upper contour intersects the lower contour shortly before the right endpoint and continues at a downward angle rather than finishing parallel. The upper endpoint also extends slightly farther right because of the rotation. If the intended result requires distinct, parallel contours at the finish, a smaller rotation or a different pivot should retain much of the widened left opening without producing overlap. No font recommendation is relevant at this contour-development stage.

## Comparison

Compared with `logo-002-002.png`, the left-side negative space is substantially larger and the right-side gap is almost entirely eliminated. Experiment 002-002 converged gently into two separate horizontal lines; experiment 002-003 creates a much stronger taper but sacrifices the parallel right finish and introduces an intersection. This preview is useful as an upper bound for how much clockwise rotation the current geometry can tolerate.
