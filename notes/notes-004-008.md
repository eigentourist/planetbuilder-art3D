# Notes — 004-008

## Interpretation

Create `PLANETBUILDER` as one Days One 3D text object, convert it to a mesh, and bake three rotations in the specified order: positive 30 degrees around X, positive 20 degrees around Z, then positive 15 degrees around Y. Rotate the guides positive 15 degrees around Y, then fit the fully rotated text mesh into the projected negative space between the yawed guides.

## Observations

The positive Z rotation produces the requested overall attitude: the left side is lower and the right side is higher from the camera's viewpoint. However, applying that rotation before the existing world-aligned bounding-box fit causes severe vertical compression. The rotated word occupies a much taller source-space Y range, so normalization into the guide gap flattens the glyphs and makes much of the title appear nearly edge-on. The result is not adequately legible.

This is the same structural limitation exposed by the earlier 45-degree Z experiment, though the 20-degree angle produces a less extreme failure. The desired roll should instead be applied after the successful envelope fitting operation, potentially with a corresponding guide rotation if the final silhouette itself should roll. Another option would be an orientation-aware fit based on the word's longitudinal and transverse axes rather than its world-aligned X/Y bounds.

The fitted mesh remains at 8,320 vertices and approximately 13,284 triangles, below the provisional 25,000-triangle target. No GLB or texture files were produced.

## Comparison

Compared with `logo-004-007.png`, experiment 004-008 lowers the pitch from 35 to 30 degrees and adds the requested positive 20-degree Z attitude, but loses the strong readability and face visibility achieved in 004-007. The yawed guides remain coherent in both experiments. Experiment 004-007 is the stronger visual result; 004-008 confirms that Z rotation must occur after fitting or be handled by a rotation-aware fitting algorithm.
