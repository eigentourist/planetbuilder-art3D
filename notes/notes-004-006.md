# Notes — 004-006

## Interpretation

Use the Blender-compatible construction order specified in the revised instructions: create `PLANETBUILDER` as one Days One 3D text object, convert it to a mesh, rotate the converted mesh 45 degrees counter-clockwise around the X axis, bake that rotation into the mesh, and finally fit the mesh into the negative space between the guides. Interpret counter-clockwise X rotation as Blender's positive 45-degree X rotation under the right-hand convention.

Retain the reduced text tessellation established during the preceding rotation test so the fitted lettering remains below the provisional 25,000-triangle limit.

## Observations

The revised process is successful. Rotating around X tilts the lettering into depth without disturbing the word's longitudinal X axis, so the continuous envelope fit preserves native character spacing and full legibility. The complete title remains inside the guides and retains the unified typographic rhythm established by the single-mesh method.

The tilt makes the 3D construction visibly meaningful even with the camera directly in front. Front and beveled surfaces receive different illumination, producing stronger depth cues across the orange lettering. The effect is especially visible on the large left-side glyphs. The result remains restrained enough that the title still reads primarily as a logo rather than as dramatically foreshortened scene geometry.

The fitted mesh contains 8,320 vertices and approximately 13,284 triangles, comfortably below the provisional 25,000-triangle target. Days One remains readable at the reduced tessellation. No GLB or texture files were produced.

Compared with `logo-004-005.png`, which rotated around Z and collapsed the word into illegible diagonal slivers during world-aligned fitting, `logo-004-006.png` preserves the full word shape while adding depth. Experiment 004-006 is the strongest result of the single-mesh fitting sequence so far.
