# Moon 001-002 Notes

## Interpretation

Preserve the geometry, material, texture orientation, camera, framing, black background, light positions, and light sizes established in moon 001-001. Increase the three area-light powers by exactly a factor of ten: key from 100 to 1000 watts, fill from 60 to 600 watts, and rim from 40 to 400 watts.

## Observations

The increased powers provide enough illumination to evaluate the moon. The desert colors, large pale formations, smaller cracks, and surface relief are clearly visible. Highlights remain soft and controlled; the preview does not show clipped-looking hot spots. The lower-left region falls into a strong but gradual shadow, while the rim contribution helps retain the spherical edge.

The moon is centered and occupies approximately one quarter of the 1200×675 frame height as intended. The visible silhouette is smooth at approximately 12,096 triangles, and no longitudinal texture seam is apparent on the camera-facing hemisphere. The combined tangent-space normal and subtle height bump produce pronounced detail at the lower hemisphere; this is visually readable at the current title-screen scale but may merit later strength adjustment depending on the desired degree of stylization.

The lighting is not excessive. If anything, the result remains somewhat subdued against the black background, leaving room to test either a modest additional exposure increase or revised light placement in a later experiment. The present illumination is nevertheless sufficient for meaningful visual iteration.

## Comparison with moon 001-001

Moon 001-001 was effectively black under the original 100/60/40-watt rig, preventing evaluation of its textures and surface response. Moon 001-002 changes only the three light powers, using 1000/600/400 watts. The new preview reveals the intended desert surface and spherical form without changing framing, geometry, material wiring, or the established key/fill/rim balance. This is a substantial improvement and establishes a usable lighting baseline for subsequent moon experiments.
