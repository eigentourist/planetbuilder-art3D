# Notes — 004-005

## Interpretation

Expand the successful single-mesh spacing workflow by rotating the complete Days One text object 45 degrees counter-clockwise before conversion. Convert the rotated text to a mesh, bake the rotation into the mesh vertices, and then fit the rotated mesh into the same negative-space envelope. Reduce text tessellation to move the resulting geometry below the provisional 25,000-triangle limit.

## Observations

The experiment demonstrates that a 45-degree source rotation is not compatible with the current bounding-box mapping method. Once rotated, the long horizontal word contributes heavily to the source mesh's vertical extent. Mapping that entire rotated bounding box into the relatively shallow guide gap compresses the glyphs into narrow diagonal slivers. The title is no longer legible as `PLANETBUILDER`.

The result is technically faithful to the requested construction order, but it does not fulfill the logo's readability requirement. If the artistic purpose of the rotation is to influence letter slant or trajectory, the fitting algorithm would need to preserve the word's own rotated longitudinal and transverse axes rather than normalizing its world-aligned X and Y bounding box. Alternatively, individual shear or italic deformation after the successful unrotated fit would provide more controllable results.

The geometry optimization was successful: the fitted mesh contains 8,320 vertices and approximately 13,284 triangles, comfortably below the provisional 25,000-triangle logo limit. The lower curve and bevel tessellation remain visually adequate at the rendered scale. No GLB or texture files were produced.

## Comparison

Experiment 004-004 failed before producing a preview because Blender could not apply rotation directly to a live text object. Compared with the preceding successful `logo-004-003.png`, experiment 004-005 preserves the improved native spacing and reduces triangle count from approximately 45,412 to 13,284, but the added 45-degree pre-rotation causes extreme vertical compression during fitting. Experiment 004-003 remains the stronger visual basis, while 004-005 establishes that the current fit must become orientation-aware before pre-rotation can be useful.
