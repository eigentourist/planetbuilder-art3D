# Notes — 002-004

## Interpretation

Repeat the upper-contour rotation experiment using 5 degrees clockwise instead of 10 degrees. Keep the same upper and lower geometry, central object-origin pivot, spacing, material, framing, and background so the effect of reducing the angle can be evaluated directly.

## Observations

The 5-degree rotation produces a much more usable result. It retains a generous opening between the lines on the left and narrows the space clearly toward the right, but the contours remain separate throughout. The right-side gap is compact and readable rather than collapsing into an intersection.

Because this remains a rigid rotation, the upper contour finishes at a mild downward angle while the lower contour finishes horizontally. The lines therefore converge cleanly but do not become parallel at their endpoints. The upper endpoint also extends slightly farther right, though less noticeably than in the 10-degree test. This may be desirable as a dynamic top contour, or it may call for a later local adjustment if a parallel finish remains important. No font recommendation is relevant at this contour-development stage.

## Comparison

Compared with `logo-002-003.png`, reducing the clockwise rotation from 10 degrees to 5 degrees removes the right-side intersection and restores useful negative space between the contours. The left opening is less extreme but remains substantially wider than in the unrotated `logo-002-002.png`. Experiment 002-004 offers a better balance between dramatic taper and structural clarity, and is the stronger basis for further refinement.
