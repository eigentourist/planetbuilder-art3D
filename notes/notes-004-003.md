# Notes — 004-003

## Interpretation

Improve Days One character spacing through the proposed three-step workflow: create `PLANETBUILDER` as one 3D text object, convert the complete word to one mesh, and then fit that mesh continuously into the negative space between the guides. Preserve the font's native glyph advances and kerning rather than positioning glyph centers independently.

The converted mesh is fitted by mapping its horizontal extent across the logo span and mapping its vertical extent into the local guide gap with a 15-percent margin at both top and bottom. This produces a continuous deformation across the full word rather than separate rotation and scaling for every character.

## Observations

The new workflow successfully addresses the principal spacing problem. `PLANETBUILDER` now reads as one coherent typographic unit, and the excessive gaps around the narrow `I` are gone. Spacing across `BUILDER` is markedly more natural because Days One's native character advances remain intact. The entire word continues to fit inside the guide envelope.

Continuous mesh fitting also gives the logo a more unified flow. Letter tops and bottoms follow the guide boundaries directly, and transitions between neighboring glyphs no longer depend on abrupt per-letter rotations. The strongest deformation occurs at the tall left side, where the initial letters become notably vertical and elongated; this is visually energetic but should be evaluated against the desired historical logo character.

The converted text mesh contains 27,264 vertices and approximately 45,412 triangles. This exceeds the provisional 25,000-triangle logo limit by a substantial margin. A later experiment should reduce text curve resolution and bevel complexity before conversion, or apply controlled mesh simplification afterward, while checking that Days One's curved silhouettes remain smooth. No GLB or texture files were produced.

## Comparison

Compared with `logo-004-002.png`, experiment 004-003 retains Days One, the same material, guides, camera, and lighting, but replaces thirteen independently positioned text objects with one fitted mesh. The earlier preview had obvious equal-center spacing gaps, especially around `I`; the new preview has much more consistent typographic rhythm. The tradeoff is that continuous envelope mapping introduces more visible glyph deformation and currently produces geometry above the intended mobile-friendly triangle budget.
